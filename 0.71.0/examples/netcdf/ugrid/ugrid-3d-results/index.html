    Active masks and ragged 3-D layers on a real flood mesh¶

    Hydrodynamic mesh results carry two kinds of structure a plain (time, layer, element) block cannot hold faithfully, and UgridDataset now models both (additively — a 2-D or uniform mesh behaves exactly as before):

    1. A per-timestep active (wet/dry) mask — a cell can be wet at one step and dry at the next; folding "dry" into NaN loses the distinction between a dry cell and a missing one.
    2. Ragged per-column vertical layers — a genuinely 3-D column carries a different number of active layers than its neighbour, so the data is a flat volume axis, not a rectangular (layer, face) block.

    This tutorial uses a real D-Flow FM flood simulation — the Manzese test case from the MDAL project (Lutra Consulting, MIT-licensed; tests/data/ugrid/D-Flow1.1/manzese_1d2d_small_map.nc). We kept its unstructured mesh2d (1 824 faces) and its real waterdepth / water-level (s1) fields over 6 output steps, and rewrote that subset through pyramids' own UGRID writer so the sample is small and self-contained.

    The flood front¶

    The simulation floods the domain over its six steps: the wet area grows from ~1 % of the cells to ~15 %. We plot the water depth at the first and last step — the dry cells (depth 0) are the white background the water spreads across.

    Part 1 — a first-class wet/dry mask (issue #1331)¶

    A cell is wet where its water depth exceeds a small drying threshold. That is a per-step boolean, distinct from "missing": a dry cell has a perfectly good value (depth ≈ 0), it is simply not part of the active flow. We derive the mask from the real depths and attach it to the variable through its first-class active field.

    In [3]:
    Copied! 
    DRYING = 0.01  # metres; below this a D-Flow FM cell is treated as dry
depths = np.asarray(ds["mesh2d_waterdepth"].data)
wet = depths > DRYING  # (n_time, n_face) boolean — a per-step mask

# `active` is a first-class field on the variable; set it in place.
ds["mesh2d_waterdepth"].active = wet

mask = ds.active_mask("mesh2d_waterdepth")
print("mask shape:", mask.shape, "(per-step: n_time x n_face)")
print("wet cells per step:", [int(w.sum()) for w in mask])

    active_mask hands back the raw boolean field — it is never applied to the values on its own. masked_values is the opt-in: it blanks dry cells to NaN in addition to the usual no-data blanking, so a caller who wants "treat dry as missing" asks for it explicitly, while the two stay distinguishable.

    The mask unlocks a wetted-area time series¶

    Because the mask is kept per step and separate from the values, we can measure the wetted area — the real reason to keep wet/dry rather than fold it into NaN. We weight each wet cell by its real face area.

    The mask rides through selections and round-trips to disk¶

    Selecting a step or a range slices the mask with the data, and writing the mesh to NetCDF keeps the mask as a companion variable — distinct from the fill value — so a read brings it back.

    Part 2 — ragged per-column vertical layers (issue #1330)¶

    pyramids' built-in reader is 2-D: a genuinely 3-D file's layers are read by a pyramids-mesh driver plugin. Here we construct a VolumeTopology on this real mesh to show the model and its accessors — deeper flooded columns get more sigma layers, shallow ones fewer, so the layering is ragged. (A real 3-D driver populates the same object from the file's own vertical grid.)

    In [7]:
    Copied! 
    # Work with the wettest step so the column depths are meaningful; every face gets at least one
# layer, deeper water gets more (clamped to 6). This is a CSR layout: offsets into a flat volume
# axis, one slice per column.
d_last = depths[last]
layer_count = np.clip(1 + np.rint(d_last / 0.2).astype(int), 1, 6)
volume_offset = np.concatenate([[0], np.cumsum(layer_count)])
topology = VolumeTopology(layer_count=layer_count, volume_offset=volume_offset)
print(
    "faces:",
    topology.n_face,
    "| volumes:",
    topology.n_volume,
    "| deepest column has",
    topology.max_layers,
    "layers",
)

# A flat (n_volume,) "salinity" that decays with layer position within each column.
layer_pos = topology.layer_of_volume()
salinity = 35.0 - 0.5 * layer_pos
vol_var = ds["mesh2d_waterdepth"].with_data(salinity)  # reuse mesh/location plumbing
vol_var.location = "volume"
vol_var.dimensions = ("mesh2d_nVolumes",)
vol_var.active = None
vol3d = UgridDataset(ds.mesh, {"salinity": vol_var}, {}, volume_topology=topology)
print("n_volume:", vol3d.n_volume, "| n_layers (max over columns):", vol3d.n_layers)

    Summary¶

    • The active mask (MeshVariable.active, UgridDataset.active_mask / masked_values) keeps per-step wet/dry status first-class and separate from nodata, so "dry" never masquerades as "missing" and quantities like a wetted-area time series stay recoverable. It rides through the shape-preserving selections and round-trips to NetCDF and xarray.
    • The ragged volume topology (VolumeTopology, location="volume", isel_layer / collapse_layer / n_volume) represents genuinely 3-D columns with different layer counts without padding, and makes absent layers explicit.

    Both are additive — a 2-D or uniform-layer mesh is unaffected. See the reference page 3-D results (masks & ragged layers) for the on-disk layout, and issues #1331 and #1330.
